Developer contract checklist (DPDP Act basics)

Before you start

Not legal advice

⚠️ Not legal advice. This is general information. Get your contract reviewed by a lawyer. The DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified in Nov 2025 and apply in phases, with most duties expected by around mid-2027. Starting now is the smart move.
Why

Why it matters (DPDP Act, 2023)

  • Whoever decides why and how personal data is used is the Data Fiduciary. If it's your website, that's you.
  • Section 8(1): you are responsible for processing done on your behalf by a Data Processor (your developer or agency), irrespective of any agreement to the contrary.
  • Failing to take reasonable security safeguards can attract a penalty of up to ₹250 crore.
  • The developer isn't automatically off the hook, but to recover your losses you need clear written terms.
Must-dos

The 3 must-dos

  1. A written contract (not a WhatsApp "bhai bana do").
  2. The domain in your name, registered with your own account and email.
  3. Admin logins with you: hosting, CMS, database, analytics and payment gateway. The developer gets their own separate user, never your master login.
Contract

What to put in the contract (checklist)

  • Scope and deliverables: pages, features, timeline, handover date.
  • Ownership: you own the code, design, content and data. Assignment of IP on payment.
  • Data protection clause: the developer acts only on your written instructions, processes data only for this project, and never shares or sells it.
  • Security standards: HTTPS, form data encrypted, admin 2FA, regular updates and backups, no plain-text passwords.
  • Access control: least-privilege access, removed at the end of the project.
  • Breach notice: the developer must tell you immediately (e.g. within 24 hours) about any breach or suspected leak, and help you respond.
  • Sub-contractors: no third party touches the data without your written OK.
  • Indemnity: the developer compensates you for losses caused by their negligence or breach of the contract.
  • Confidentiality: survives after the project ends.
  • Handover and deletion: full code and credentials handed over; the developer deletes all copies of customer data at the end and confirms it in writing.
  • Maintenance terms: who patches security issues, for how long, and at what cost.
  • Jurisdiction and dispute resolution.
Owner basics

Your own basics (as the owner)

  • Collect only the data you need on forms.
  • Add a clear privacy notice and consent checkbox.
  • Know where form data is stored (email, sheet, database) and who can see it.
  • Keep a simple log of who has access.
Prompt

Claude prompt to draft your contract

Copy karo
Draft a website development agreement between <my business> (client, India) and <developer>.
Include: scope, timeline, payment milestones, IP ownership to client, a data processing clause aligned with India's DPDP Act 2023 (developer = data processor acting on client instructions), security standards, 24-hour breach notification, no sub-processors without consent, indemnity, confidentiality, handover of code and credentials, deletion of personal data at the end, and jurisdiction <city>.
Use simple English and add a one-page summary in Hinglish.
⚠️ Then have a lawyer review it before signing.
